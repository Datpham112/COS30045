A 55-inch TV can use as much power as a 75-inch

Screen size sets the baseline for a TV's running cost, but the star rating decides how far above or below that baseline you land. This story uses the energy labels of 2,867 TVs registered in Australia to show what to check before you buy.

Bigger screens cost more to run

Energy use climbs steadily with screen size. The typical 43-inch TV on the register is labelled at 231 kWh a year, about $66. The typical 85-inch TV is labelled at 768 kWh, about $219, or more than three times as much.

Size and labelled energy use are closely linked across the whole dataset (a correlation of about 0.86), so size is the first thing to decide.

But TVs of the same size can be far apart

Each bar runs from the lowest to the highest labelled energy use among TVs of that size, and the dark dash marks the median. The bars are long at every size.

Among 55-inch TVs the range is 170 to 817 kWh a year. That is a gap of about $185 a year, larger than the $120 difference between the typical 55-inch and the typical 85-inch.

Star rating explains the gap, technology does not

Star ratings are calculated relative to screen size, so within one size they work as an efficiency score. Among 55-inch TVs, those rated 6 stars or more have a median of 256 kWh a year. Those rated 1 to 2.5 stars have a median of 596 kWh, slightly more than a typical 75-inch TV at 578 kWh.

Screen technology makes little difference at this size. The median 55-inch TV is labelled at 339 kWh for LCD, 339 for LED-backlit LCD and 353 for OLED.

Only 11 of the 470 55-inch TVs fall in the lowest star band, so treat that bar as indicative rather than exact.

What to check before you buy

  1. Choose the smallest screen that suits your room, because size has the biggest effect on running cost.
  2. Compare star ratings only between TVs of a similar size. A 5-star 43-inch and a 5-star 85-inch use very different amounts of energy.
  3. Read the kWh per year on the label and multiply it by your own tariff from your electricity bill.
